# Professional assets

Load for Combined, Positioning, JD fit, or when multiple assets are in play.

## Source of truth

Build or maintain an **achievement inventory** (ADM rows). Every public asset is
an adaptation of that inventory—not a separate invented career.

| Asset | Job | Density |
|-------|-----|---------|
| **CV / resume** | Relevant proof for a target opportunity | Tight bullets; Action+Context+Result |
| **LinkedIn** | Narrative + discoverability + identity | Headline/About story; searchable experience |
| **Portfolio** | How they think and deliver | Few case studies; problem→judgment→outcome |
| **Interview** | Live competency evidence | STAR stories mapped to competencies |

Reuse evidence; rewrite for purpose. Identical copy-paste across assets is a smell.

## CV / resume

- Reverse chronological unless a market/employer clearly expects otherwise
- Skim in seconds: role targets, latest impact, clarity
- Prefer accomplishment bullets over duty lists
- Length and identity fields follow `regional-markets.md`
- Tailor to opportunity without inventing fit
- Sound human and specific—no AI slogan salad (see Speech / anti-slop)
- **Risk-readable:** show the problem, scope, and outcome so the reader needn’t invent how you fit

### Timeline (gaps, layoffs, short stints)

- Keep the CV clean; don’t lead with a defensive essay about why you left
- Honest dates/employers; never stretch or invent employment to hide a gap
- Optional: year-only dates when market-appropriate and consistent across assets
- Short stints: lead with what you delivered; save leave-reasons for interview
- Layoff / restructuring: factual when asked; don’t over-explain on the page
- Career change: keep official past titles; reframe transferable scope in bullets—don’t invent new titles

## LinkedIn

Documented completeness themes: headline, About, experience, skills, media,
recommendations—not a ranking guarantee.

Coach:

- Headline = professional identity + searchable truth (not a slogan salad)
- About = positioning narrative grounded in real work
- Experience aligned with CV dates/titles; longer form OK
- Skills = real capabilities recruiters Boolean for
- Flag CV↔LinkedIn inconsistencies
- **Between jobs:** if consulting/freelance/study is real, list it with honest dates; don’t invent a fake current employer. Sabbatical/care labels only if true and they can say one calm sentence about them

## Portfolio

From NYU Tandon + Intuit-as-example:

- Goal is usually **land the interview**, not replace it
- Dual audience: recruiter skim first, hiring-manager depth second
- Quality > quantity (often 3–5 strong pieces)
- Per project: summary (problem/solution/outcome), role, process, impact, tools
- Only projects they can discuss at length
- Contact path obvious; prune unfinished/weak work

Adapt case-study shape to non-design domains (PM write-ups, campaign retros,
architecture decision records presented for humans, etc.).

## Interview stories

- STAR from achievement inventory
- Tag stories to likely competencies from the JD
- Keep individual contribution clear inside team wins
- Prepare probes: why that approach, what you’d do differently, how measured
- Timeline probes: gap, short stint, layoff, CV↔LinkedIn mismatch—one factual line, then steer back to value. Don’t ramble or panic-script

## Positioning / market packaging

When materials are fine but responses are cold, check the **target market**, not
only the prose: wrong seniority band, full-time when interim/fractional/advisory
fits the evidence better, or title language that doesn’t match how buyers search.
Repackage the same inventory; don’t invent a new career. Stay inside branding
scope—no life-coaching detour unless they clearly ask and stay evidence-bound.

## Consistency checklist

- Dates, titles, employers
- Seniority claims vs evidenced scope
- Metrics that appear in one asset but not another without reason
- Same project with conflicting “led” vs “contributed”
- Education: completed vs attended vs in progress—wording that survives a background check
- After fixing a mismatch: confirm they can explain it calmly if asked
