## Achievement writing

**Action + Project + Result / X by Y by Z (Yale)** — Bullets show contribution and
impact, not task lists.
→ Rewrite only after Problem/Contribution/Outcome (and Evidence when claimed).
→ https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/writing-impactful-resume-bullets/

**PAR (MIT)** — Activity + Project + Result; quantify via money, %, scale.
→ Prefer real metrics; if none, use scope/adoption or honest qualitative outcome.
→ https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes-writing-about-your-skills/

**Action + Context = Results (UC Davis)** — Ordinary duties hide accomplishments.
→ Use discovery questions before assuming the candidate has nothing to show.
→ https://careercenter.ucdavis.edu/resumes-and-materials/resumes/accomplishment-statements

**ACE (Princeton)** — Action, Context, End result; strong verbs as vocabulary.
→ Verb lists help wording; they do not justify inflated ownership.
→ https://careerdevelopment.princeton.edu/resume-guide/action-oriented-accomplishment-statements

**Europass** — Clear reverse-chronological experience; tailor; readable language.
→ Use when EU mobility/public programs expect it; otherwise prefer market-local CVs.
→ https://europass.europa.eu/en/create-europass-cv?lang=en

## Impact and seniority

**Dropbox impact** — Impact = customer/business win; Consistency, Velocity,
Accountability; multiple levers and archetypes.
→ Ask who benefited, what changed, how often, what they owned—not title inflation.
→ https://dropbox.github.io/dbx-career-framework/what_is_impact.html

**Monzo progression** — Compass not GPS; scope ladder; multi-path senior shapes;
no scoring algorithms.
→ Translate to portable Scope/Influence/Autonomy; never certify “you are Staff.”
→ https://monzo.com/documents/engineering-progression-framework-v3-0.pdf
→ https://github.com/monzo/progression-framework

**GitLab career matrix** — Cumulative competencies; scope of decision rights;
leadership + domain mastery.
→ Brand seniority as what they own and whom they unblock—not GitLab jargon.
→ https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/careers/matrix/

## Selection and interviews

**CIPD selection** — Structured, job-related criteria; fairness; multi-method
assessment.
→ Map materials to competencies with evidence; prepare for interview + work sample.
→ https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/selection-factsheet/

**OPM structured interviews** — Behavioral past behavior; situational dilemmas;
shared rating anchors; probes.
→ Build STAR stories tagged to role competencies; do not invent “secret federal Qs.”
→ https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/structured-interviews/guide/

**Yale STAR** — Situation, Task, Action, Result; quantify when possible.
→ Interview prep reuses the achievement inventory; individual contribution clear in team stories.
→ https://ocs.yale.edu/channels/the-behavioral-interview/

## ATS and search

**Greenhouse parse failures** — Large files, graphics/photos/word art, image uploads,
tables, columns, headers/footers, text boxes, spaced letters, unclear sections.
→ Prefer single-column, selectable text, contact in body, standard headings.
→ Cite as Greenhouse-documented; do not claim every ATS behaves identically.
→ https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse

**Lever parsing** — Supported office/PDF/HTML/ODF types; cannot parse image files;
highlight-text test; name/org/contact extraction.
→ Official docs are thin on columns/tables—label layout advice as general practice + Greenhouse certainty, not Lever-guaranteed rules.
→ https://help.lever.co/hc/en-us/articles/20087345054749-Understanding-resume-parsing

**LinkedIn profile completeness** — Headline, About, experience, skills, photo,
recommendations help a usable profile.
→ Completeness ≠ recruiter ranking promise.
→ https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a554351

**LinkedIn Boolean** — AND/OR/NOT, quotes, parentheses; no wildcards in documented operators.
→ Put true title/skill synonyms in profile fields and prose—no stuffing.
→ https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a524335

**LinkedIn AI Search** — NL prompts → filters; can match hard-to-list traits from prose.
→ Write plain-language evidence of competencies, not vanity SEO scores.
→ https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a1659757

## Portfolios

**NYU Tandon** — Dual audience (recruiter skim, then HM craft); quality > quantity;
case-study story; portfolio lands the interview.
→ Prune weak projects; skim-first structure; speak 15–20 min per piece.
→ https://engineering.nyu.edu/life-tandon/experiential-learning-center/building-portfolio

**Intuit SWE portfolio** — 3–5 projects with problem, stack, demo; living document.
→ Profession-specific example; adapt structure to other domains.
→ https://www.intuit.com/blog/global-stories/software-engineer-portfolio/

## Regional pointers

**France (service-public)** — CV guidance exists; photo optional; prefer job-relevant
data only (CNIL spirit).
→ Ask target market before recommending photo/age/family fields.
→ https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33336

**UK vs US naming** — UK “CV” (often 1–2 pages); US industry “resume” (often 1 page
early career); both typically omit photo/age/marital status.
→ Match spelling, page size, and length to target country.

**Morocco / North Africa** — Common practice often includes photo and more personal
fields; French common for business roles; diplomas weighted.
→ Weaker official documentation—label as common practice; strip extras when targeting FR/UK/US.

## Speech (operational summary)

**Peer-coach speech** — Concrete first; one plain claim; ground tips in their
materials; keep internal methodology labels internal; no AI fog.
→ Enforced in `SKILL.md` ## Speech on every turn.

**AI-tell avoidance** — Significance inflation, chatbot manners, sycophancy,
hedge theater, generic pep endings (patterns catalogued widely, e.g. Wikipedia
“Signs of AI writing”).
→ Strip from coach chat and rewritten professional copy; never invent facts for voice.
→ https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing
